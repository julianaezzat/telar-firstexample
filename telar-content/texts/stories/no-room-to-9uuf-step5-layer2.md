---
title: "Go deeper"
---

Other Concerned Students flyers connected library access to the cost of education. One message told students to "study in your library that you pay for," linking study space directly to student fees and university accountability.

This mattered because UC student fees were rising sharply in the early 1990s. A 1995 California Legislative Analyst's Office report noted that UC undergraduate fees had increased by 67 percent since 1991-92. Against that backdrop, students were not only asking for a quiet place to study. They were questioning what the university owed them as the cost of public education increased. The demand for library access became part of a larger argument: if students were paying more, they deserved meaningful access to the resources that made their education possible.
